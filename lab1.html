List of Orchid Plants

  • Amerorchis rotundifolia

    Other Names: Small Round Leaved Orchis

    Location: Newfoundland, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories

    Specific Habitat: In Ottawa District, fairly open treed fens, open sedge fens, and some swamps. In Bruce Peninsula, prefers cold northern forests and shaded bogs and swamps.

    Flowering Season: June to early July

    Description: Height to 15 cm. Flowers white to pale pink with deep purple spots. Petals form a hood. Leaf solitary, dull yellow-green.

    Comments: Widespread but local in the east. Only two locations in the Bruce Peninsula, rare in Ottawa District. Highly variable lip coloration.

    References: Guide to Orchids of North America, Orchids of Ontario, The Orchids of Bruce&Grey

  • Platanthera dilatata

    Other Names: Tall White Bog Orchid, Fragrant White Orchid, Bog Candle

    Location: Newfoundland, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories

    Specific Habitat: Common in the northwest mountains of North America in sunny wet terrain. In the east, mainly a plant of the Boreal and Mixed Forest Regions. Can be found in fens in areas of calcareous bedrock.

    Flowering Season: June to July, and as late as September.

    Description: Height 15-65 cm. Leaves linear to lanceolate, dark green, up to 25 x 5 cm, gradually reduced to bracts upwards. Raceme 12-60 flowers. Flowers white, vanilla or clove scented.

    Comments: Occurs only in open sedge fens and a few adjacent treed fens. Never grows in more acidic environments. Often found with Sedges, Pitcher Plant, and Sundews, and with orchids like Pogonia ophioglossoides and Platanthera hyperborea.

    References: Orchids of Blue and Grey, Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1

  • Calypso bulbosa

    Other Names: Fairy Slipper

    Location: Newfoundland, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories. Primarily a Canadian-Alaskan orchid. Rare and local in both Eurasia and North America.

    Specific Habitat: In the east, often found in inaccessible, damp forests. In the Ottawa District, the plants grow in calcareous cedar swamps in partially open areas in moist needle-mould. In the west, it occurs at moderately high elevations in the Rocky Mountains, often in dry pine forests.

    Flowering Season: May-June-July

    Description: Height up to 20 cm depending on habitat. Leaf: solitary, basal, dark to bluish-green, appears in autumn. Flower: showy, solitary, pinkish-purple, rarely whitish. Lip is saccate, white, with yellowish 2-pronged apex, streaked with reddish-brown inside. Apron formed from edges of lip is white with pinkish-purple spots.

    Comments: This orchid has been largely reduced to remote colonies due to habitat destruction. White-flowered f. candida has been reported in specific areas. Var. occidentalis is debated as a separate species or subspecies, found in the Pacific Northwest.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, Orchids of the Northeast, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Native Orchids of the United States and Canada excluding Florida, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Cypripedium candidum

    Other Names: Small White Lady's-slipper

    Location: Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan

    Specific Habitat: Found in marly soil of fens, shade intolerant. Decreasing due to invasion by woody shrubs. In southern Ontario, now only found in specific regions such as delta islands of Lake St. Clair and Stoco Fen in Hastings County.

    Flowering Season: Late May to early June

    Description: Height up to 25 cm. Leaves 3-4, elliptic to lanceolate. Sepals and petals are greenish, often red-striped, with twisted petals. Lip white with violet veins inside, sometimes purple-spotted near the orifice. Yellow staminode with purple spots.

    Comments: The rarest of the lady's-slippers in Ontario, *Cypripedium candidum* has declined due to wetland drainage and prairie conversion. Annual mowing has been shown to help maintain its populations.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist, Vol III - 1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Spiranthes romanzoffiana

    Other Names: Oval Ladies' Tresses

    Location: Newfoundland, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories

    Specific Habitat: Widespread in boreal and cool temperate regions of North America. Common in the northern part of the Bruce Peninsula but infrequent in the Ottawa Region. Prefers sunny, often marly places or areas that flood early in the season.

    Flowering Season: Mid July to early September, occasionally later.

    Description: Height 7-25 cm. Leaves linear to oblong-lanceolate, mostly basal, becoming smaller higher up the stem. Inflorescence dense and 3-ranked. Flowers are tubular, white or creamy, and fragrant. Petals and sepals form a hood over the lip, which is fiddle-shaped and constricted at about two-thirds of the way from base to tip.

    Comments: Widespread from Lakes Erie and Ontario to James and Hudson’s Bays. Frequent in the Canadian Shield region. Colonies are small and short-lived. Often found with grasses, horsetails, sedges, and mosses, and occasionally alongside *Platanthera dilatata*.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Native Orchids of the United States and Canada excluding Florida, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Corallorhiza striata

    Location: Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia

    Specific Habitat: Found in dry to moist clear floors in coniferous and deciduous forests, particularly in Ontario. Most frequent on the Bruce Peninsula and Manitoulin Island in dry cedar woodlands with limestone near the surface. In the Ottawa area, it grows in partial sun in cedar-dominated areas. Also found in dappled to deep shade in mixed forests with substrates of sand, occasionally clay, and in mossy, organic soil of swamps.

    Flowering Season: Mid-June to early July. The first of the *Corallorhiza* species to bloom.

    Description: Height up to 45 cm. Stem erect, stout, leafless, madder-purple to brownish-purple. Raceme is a loose group of drooping flowers. Flowers are pinkish-yellow or white, striped with reddish-purple. Petals form a loose hood with the dorsal sepal, and the lip is tongue-shaped and pendant.

    Comments: One of the most striking *Corallorhiza* species, identifiable by its distinctive red-striped flowers. The flowers are small but sparkling. It is primarily a western species, often found with *Corallorhiza maculata*.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III - 1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Aplectrum hyemale

    Other Names: Puttyroot

    Location: Quebec, Ontario

    Specific Habitat: Found in rich deciduous forests on moist, shaded slopes.

    Flowering Season: May to June

    Description: Leaves green, ovate, developing in fall and persisting through winter. Flowers small, brownish-purple.

    Comments: This species is listed as endangered in some regions.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Arethusa bulbosa

    Other Names: Dragon's Mouth Orchid

    Location: Newfoundland, Quebec, Ontario

    Specific Habitat: Found in bogs and wet meadows.

    Flowering Season: June

    Description: Bright pink flowers, solitary, on short stems. Leaves are absent at flowering time.

    Comments: Common in the northern part of its range but declining elsewhere.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Calopogon tuberosus

    Other Names: Grass Pink Orchid

    Location: Newfoundland, Ontario, Manitoba

    Specific Habitat: Open wetlands, bogs, and fens.

    Flowering Season: June to July

    Description: Pink to purple flowers, arranged in a loose spike.

    Comments: Often found growing alongside other bog-loving orchids.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Cephalanthera austinae

    Other Names: Phantom Orchid

    Location: British Columbia

    Specific Habitat: Found in deep, shaded forests.

    Flowering Season: June to July

    Description: Pure white flowers with no leaves, growing on a single stalk.

    Comments: Threatened due to habitat destruction.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Cypripedium acaule

    Other Names: Pink Lady’s Slipper

    Location: Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba

    Specific Habitat: Acidic pine forests and sandy soils.

    Flowering Season: May to June

    Description: Large pink flowers, with a pouch-like lip. Leaves are basal, large, and leathery.

    Comments: One of the most widespread lady’s slippers, yet sensitive to environmental changes.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Cypripedium parviflorum

    Other Names: Yellow Lady's Slipper

    Location: Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan

    Specific Habitat: Found in rich woodlands, fens, and moist prairies.

    Flowering Season: May to July

    Description: Yellow flowers with twisted brown sepals. Leaves are large and clasp the stem.

    Comments: This species is common in the prairies but has declined in other regions.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Epipactis helleborine

    Other Names: Broad-leaved Helleborine

    Location: Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia

    Specific Habitat: Found in disturbed areas, often near human development, such as roadsides and parks.

    Flowering Season: July to September

    Description: Green to purple flowers in a dense spike. Leaves are broad and clasp the stem.

    Comments: This non-native orchid is invasive in some regions.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Goodyera pubescens

    Other Names: Downy Rattlesnake Plantain

    Location: Ontario, Quebec

    Specific Habitat: Found in dry, sandy forests, especially in pine-dominated areas.

    Flowering Season: July to August

    Description: Small white flowers arranged in a dense spiral along the spike. Leaves are basal with a distinctive white reticulation.

    Comments: One of the most common orchids in eastern North America.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey

  • Pogonia ophioglossoides

    Other Names: Rose Pogonia

    Location: Ontario, Quebec, Newfoundland

    Specific Habitat: Found in bogs, fens, and wet meadows.

    Flowering Season: June to July

    Description: Pink flowers with a fringed lip, growing on a short stem. Leaves are lance-shaped and alternate.

    Comments: This orchid is common in boggy habitats and is often found growing alongside other wetland plants.

    References: Orchids of Ontario, The Canadian Field-Naturalist Vol III-1, The Orchids of Bruce & Grey
